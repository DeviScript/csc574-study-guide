# Exam Philosophy

The instructor describes the exams using the following principles:

**Covered content:** The exam is intended to be comprehensive and include tasks for each major covered topic. Rather than one monolithic essay, expect a number of tasks, generally requiring at most a few sentences per answer.

**Task type:** The majority of tasks are generally short written answers ranging from 1-2 words to 2-3 sentences. Some tasks may require sketching or labeling diagrams.

**Quick Questions:** A likely task format is a “Quick Questions” task with subquestions requiring answers from about one word to one sentence, covering a wide range of lecture areas.

**Writing code:** The instructor does not consider a written exam a good place to verify coding knowledge. At most, expect short coding tasks using pseudocode, with possible exceptions for specific implementation details discussed in lecture.

**Expectations:** The task text generally states what is expected and how grading is considered.

“For scenario X provide and describe 2 attacks (1 name and 1–2 sentences description each, 2 points each) and 1 defense (1 name and 1–2 sentences description, 2 points).”

# Expected Task Types: Bloom’s Taxonomy

The course describes exam tasks as roughly classifiable along Bloom’s Taxonomy. Because this is a special topics course, tasks tend toward the upper levels, while still relying on lower-level knowledge as a foundation.

1.  **Remember:** Recall foundational or factual information: names, dates, formulas, definitions, components, or methods.

2.  **Understand:** Explain main ideas and concepts and make meaning by interpreting, classifying, summarizing, inferring, comparing, and explaining.

3.  **Apply:** Recognize or use concepts in real-world situations and address when, where, or how to employ methods and ideas.

4.  **Analyze:** Break a topic or idea into components or examine a subject from different perspectives.

5.  **Synthesize:** Consider individual elements together to draw conclusions, identify themes, or determine common elements.

6.  **Evaluate:** Make judgments based on criteria and standards. This often requires checking and critiquing an argument or concept and supporting a judgment with reasons and evidence; there may not be one clearly correct answer.

7.  **Create:** Put elements together to form a coherent or functional whole, including reorganizing elements into a new pattern or structure through planning.

## 1. Security Fundamentals

_Security terminology, goals, and measurement challenges_

- **Explain** definitions of security.

- **Define** adversary, trust, threat, and security models.

  > Provide a definition you encountered in class for the term “security model”.
  >
  > A security model consists of which other models?

- **Define** confidentiality, integrity, availability, asset, participant, vulnerability, threat, attack, defense, TCB, and risk.

  > Would you say a participant in a security model is limited to the actual people involved (user, attacker, etc.)?
  >
  > Define the 3 components of the CIA triad.
  >
  > Is a broken window in your server room considered under the term “threat”?
  >
  > What does TCB stand for?

- **Identify** common attacker types, four attack archetypes, and five defense archetypes.

  > List and define at least 3 out of the five archetype defenses that we discussed in the lecture.
  >
  > What attack archetypes match for a ransom ware attack scenario (attacker accesses servers and deploys ransom ware that encrypts all files, causing an interruption for all services that rely on those files)?

- **Differentiate** the concepts of trusted and trustworthy.

  > Outline the difference of trusted and trustworthy.
  >
  > For an ATM on Glenwood Ave., is its operating system trusted or trustworthy? Why?

- **Create** and articulate security models for well-understood systems.

  > Considering the ATM at Glenwood Ave from the lecture: what factors would you consider for a security model (both in terms of threats and trust).
  >
  > Assume an ATM provider installs the number pads on their machines upside down (Numbers that are normally at the bottom are now at the top) to deter PIN stealing by shoulder surfing. Do you think this is a good approach to deter PIN theft? Can you come up with any better solutions to shoulder surfing?

- **Define** the principle of adequate protection.

# How the Exam Format Shapes Preparation

Based on the instructor’s stated exam philosophy, preparation should support several kinds of recall and reasoning rather than only long-form explanation. In particular, the exam description indicates that students should be prepared to:

- give concise definitions and distinctions using course terminology;

- recognize attacks, defenses, components, and concepts in short scenarios;

- apply concepts to concrete systems and justify choices briefly;

- sketch or label diagrams when a task calls for it;

- answer broad “Quick Questions” efficiently across many lecture areas; and

- use first-half foundations on the cumulative final even when the task emphasis shifts toward the second half.

**Important:** The example tasks in this document are illustrative examples from the supplied course page, not a comprehensive list of what will appear on an exam.
