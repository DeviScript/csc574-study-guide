Symmetric cryptography foundations

Symmetric cryptography uses the same key for encryption and decryption. It is generally fast, but the communicating parties must share key material securely.

Stream and block ciphers are tools with different operating assumptions. Neither provides a complete security solution by itself.

Stream ciphers

A stream cipher combines plaintext with a pseudorandom keystream, commonly using XOR. A nonce or IV may make each generated stream different, but it must not be reused with the same key.

Keystream reuse exposes relationships between plaintexts, and stream ciphers do not provide integrity by default.

Block ciphers

A block cipher is a keyed permutation over fixed-size blocks. Substitution and permutation create confusion, diffusion, and the avalanche effect.

DES and 3DES are obsolete. AES is the modern block cipher emphasized in the lecture. Padding may be needed when data does not fill a complete block.

Modes of operation

  • ECB: simple and parallel, but repeated plaintext blocks reveal patterns.
  • CBC: hides patterns with chaining, but encryption is sequential and errors can propagate.
  • CTR: supports parallelism and random access, but nonce or counter reuse is catastrophic.

ECB, CBC, and CTR provide confidentiality only. Integrity requires authentication or an authenticated encryption construction.
